The 4Cs after KCSIE 2026

One page mapping the four areas of online risk to what AI incidents look like in school. KCSIE 2026 applies from 1 September 2026.

Whichever C an incident sits under, the staff route is identical: listen → don’t look or copy → note → tell the DSL. The 4Cs help the DSL, not the first responder.

Area of riskWhat it coversAI examples in school
ContentA child sees something harmfulAI-generated violent or sexual material; convincing fakes and misinformation; chatbot answers that encourage harm
ContactA child interacts with someone — or something — harmful. KCSIE 2026 includes generative AI that simulates human conversationCompanion apps simulating friendship or romance; chatbots encouraging secrecy or isolation; adults using AI to approach children
ConductBehaviour by or towards a child. KCSIE 2026 includes explicit images generated using AI“Nudes and semi-nudes” — altered and wholly AI-made (deepfakes) included; “nudify” apps used even “as a joke”; AI-assisted bullying
CommerceA child is exploited financiallySextortion — threats and demands for money over images; AI-built scams; pressure to pay inside companion apps
!

Sextortion: go to the DSL before the pupil goes home. Risk of self-harm: call 999.

Also statutory in KCSIE 2026

  • Filtering and monitoring reviewed at least once every academic year, with records kept.
  • The DSL’s role includes understanding the school’s filtering and monitoring systems.
