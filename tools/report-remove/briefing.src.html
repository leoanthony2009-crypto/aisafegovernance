Immediate action required: Call 999 if

the child says they will hurt themselves, or you think they might; an adult is on their way to meet them; or someone is threatening to harm them now.

Then tell the DSL.

Any other concern: tell the DSL straight away

Tell [DSL name] on [phone or office number]

In person or by phone, not by email. If you cannot reach the DSL or deputy and a child is at risk, call children's social care or the police yourself (see Contacts).

What to do Four steps, in this order

  1. 1Step 1. Listen and reassure

    • “Thank you for telling me.”
    • “You are not in trouble.”
    • “This is not your fault.”
    • “I have to tell the DSL. They will help you.”

    Do not promise to keep it secret, and do not blame anyone.

  2. 2Step 2. Do not look or copy

    • Do look away if you are shown an image, and keep any device you are handed safe for the DSL, unopened.
    • Do not ask to see it, forward, save or screenshot it, or open, share or delete it on your own device.
    • Do not question the child in detail, confront anyone or contact parents.

  3. 3Step 3. Note what you heard

    The child's own words, any usernames, the app or website, and the time. Do not look at the device to get these.

    Never a copy of the image.

  4. 4Step 4. Tell the DSL now

    Record it the same day on [our safeguarding system]; no login? Give the DSL a signed, dated note.

    Seen the image by accident? Tell the DSL and ask for support. Do not discuss it with other staff, pupils or parents.

    The DSL takes it from here; you may be asked to write down what you heard. Affected by it? Tell the DSL or [staff wellbeing contact].

SituationWhat to do
Important: threats or demands for money (sextortion) Tell the child to stop replying and block, and not to pay or send anything more; paying rarely stops the threats. Do not delete messages or accounts yet, and do not collect or screenshot anything yourself. Go to the DSL before the child goes home. Risk of suicide or self-harm: call 999.
AI-made or altered images, even “as a joke”; a pupil who received or forwarded one; a threat to share one Same four steps, for every child involved. An AI image counts exactly as a real one: making or sharing a sexual image of anyone under 18 is a criminal offence. A threatened image can often be stopped before it is posted; the DSL knows how.
The image involves a member of staff or a volunteer Tell the headteacher, not the person; the headteacher involves the LADO and the police. About the headteacher: tell the chair of governors. If you cannot do either: the LADO (see Contacts) or the NSPCC whistleblowing helpline, 0800 028 0285.

Report Remove A pupil can ask for an image to be taken down

A free Childline and Internet Watch Foundation service for under-18s in the UK, at childline.org.uk/remove, for nude or sexual images of them that are online, including AI fakes. Tell the DSL first: the DSL decides with the pupil whether and when to use it, and who supports them. The pupil makes the report themselves. Never view, upload or report the image for them. Removal is not guaranteed, and it is not the whole response. The DSL’s page has the detail, and the routes for anyone it cannot help.

[School name]Nude or sexual image of a pupil · DSL page

For the DSL Back of sheet. Decide from what you are told; do not view imagery to decide.

Refer immediately to the police and/or children's social care if any of these applies

  1. An adult is involved.
  2. There is reason to believe the child was coerced, blackmailed or groomed, or there are concerns about their capacity to consent, for example because of a special educational need.
  3. What you have been told about the imagery suggests acts that are unusual for the child's developmental stage, or violent.
  4. The imagery involves sexual acts and any child in it is under 13.
  5. The child is at immediate risk of harm, for example presenting as suicidal or self-harming.

If none applies, decide the response using the UKCIS advice, with the headteacher where appropriate, and record the reasons. If in doubt, seek advice from children's social care or the LADO.

Parents and carers

Inform and involve them at an early stage, unless that would put the child at risk. A decision not to inform them is made with children's social care or the police, and recorded.

Devices and imagery

  • Do not view imagery to make the decision. If viewing is unavoidable, it is done by the DSL or headteacher, with another member of staff present, never on a personal device, and recorded with the reasons.
  • Searching a device is a decision for the DSL or headteacher under the DfE searching guidance, and is recorded.
  • If the police are, or may become, involved: do not delete anything, disconnect the device from Wi-Fi and data, switch it off and keep it secure for them.
  • If the imagery is only on the child's own device and no referral is needed, the child may be asked to delete it; confirm and record.

Report Remove

The pupil goes to childline.org.uk/remove, creates a Childline account and reports the image to the IWF. From age 13 they can prove their age with ID (passport, provisional licence or CitizenCard), which lets the IWF remove from more sites; under-13s skip that step. Updates arrive in their Childline locker. It cannot reach encrypted apps such as WhatsApp, or copies saved on a device.

  • Decide with the pupil whether and when to use it, and who supports them. A younger child needs an adult alongside; the adult never looks at the image.
  • The account, report and locker are the pupil’s own. Staff never view, upload or report the image for them, and never use a school device or account to do so.
  • Record that it was discussed and what was decided. Removal is not the safeguarding response; the referral decision above still stands.

Not eligible? Over 18, a staff member, or an image threatened but not yet posted: Take It Down, takeitdown.ncmec.org (under 18 when taken, any country, no account). Adults: Revenge Porn Helpline 0345 6000 459 or stopncii.org.

Record and follow up

  • Record what was reported, the decision, the reasons and who was told, on [our safeguarding system], the same day.
  • Support any member of staff who saw the imagery. Remind staff that the incident is not discussed with colleagues, pupils or parents.
  • Review the case and this sheet at the next safeguarding review; next review [month year].
